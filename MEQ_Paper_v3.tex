\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The Minimum Energy Quantum (\meq{}) framework is a phenomenological proposal in which matter and
gravity are hypothesized to arise from collective behavior of a single species of massless,
light-speed constituents. We set out its assumptions and a schematic kinetic description, and
identify the additional dynamical input needed to derive, rather than assume, an effective metric
and its coupling to matter. In particular, the Einstein equation is presented as a possible
low-energy matching condition, not as a result established by the model. We discuss four
conditional experimental signatures: a short-range Yukawa correction to gravity; an additional
contribution to vacuum birefringence; a possible exchange-associated mass drift; and a
phase-correlated galactic signal. For the first two, we give illustrative sensitivity estimates
using idealized geometries and explicitly stated normalizations. The latter two are conjectural:
the mapping from microscopic exchange to an observable drift, and a quantitative galactic
prediction, remain unspecified. Existing tests constrain these parameterizations but do not
validate the proposed substrate. The framework therefore serves as a research program whose
viability depends on constructing a closed microscopic theory, matching it to established
low-energy physics, and testing its conditional signatures.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum gravity; emergent gravity; induced gravity; kinetic theory;
equivalence principle; dark matter; experimental tests

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem}

Quantum field theory (QFT) and general relativity (GR) are central, experimentally successful
frameworks whose standard formulations do not yet provide a complete quantum description of
dynamical spacetime. QFT can be formulated on curved backgrounds, while GR makes the metric
dynamical; reconciling these descriptions remains an open problem. Perturbative quantization of the
Einstein--Hilbert action is non-renormalizable by power counting, while several structured programs ---
string/M-theory, loop quantum gravity, asymptotic safety, causal dynamical triangulations,
holography --- continue to investigate possible resolutions; no approach is uniquely selected by
experimental evidence \cite{Rovelli2015,Polchinski1998,Reuter1998,Loll2020,Batista2025}.

Three empirical facts sharpen the problem rather than resolving it:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Free fall is highly universal.} MICROSCOPE constrained the differential
acceleration of Ti and Pt test bodies to the $10^{-15}$ level
\cite{Touboul2022}; lunar laser ranging also tests the response of gravitational self-energy
\cite{Williams2004}. These results constrain departures from universal coupling.
\item \textbf{Gravitational waves propagate at a speed consistent with $c$.} The joint
gravitational-wave and gamma-ray observation GW170817 places a strong bound on the difference
between the propagation speeds \cite{GW170817}. This result alone does not establish the
microscopic nature or full polarization content of a gravitational degree of freedom.
\item \textbf{Photon propagation is tightly constrained.} Gamma-ray observations bound specified
forms of energy-dependent dispersion, with limits depending on the assumed propagation model
\cite{Abdo2009,Vasileiou2013}. These bounds constrain, but do not exclude every possible,
interaction with a substrate.
\end{enumerate}
These observations motivate constraints on any substrate model; they do not establish that such a
substrate exists. Here they are used as benchmarks rather than as evidence for the proposal.

\subsection{Historical lineage}

The idea that gravity arises from the collective behavior of a more fundamental medium has a long
and productive history:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Le Sage (1784)} and Fatio de Duillier before him proposed ``push gravity'' from
an isotropic flux of corpuscles, with bodies shielding each other from the flux. Analyses of
inelastic interception raise severe heating concerns, while Laplace-type aberration arguments
constrain non-covariant retarded-flux models \cite{LeSage2002,Poincare1908}. These objections apply
to particular flux models. Elastic scattering by itself does not resolve them; a viable model
must also demonstrate energy balance, small drag, and stable orbital dynamics.
\item \textbf{Sakharov (1968)} proposed that an Einstein--Hilbert term can be induced by quantum
fields in a curved-space effective action \cite{Sakharov1968}. This is one motivation for
effective-field-theory treatments of gravity, not a derivation of the present substrate
\cite{Donoghue1994}.
\item \textbf{Jacobson (1995)} derived the Einstein equation as an equation of state of local
Rindler horizons \cite{Jacobson1995}; \textbf{Verlinde (2010)} recast gravity as entropic
\cite{Verlinde2011}; \textbf{Volovik} and the analog-gravity program demonstrated that Lorentzian
spacetime, horizons, and effective metrics arise generically as low-energy phenomena of
condensed substrates (superfluid $^3$He) \cite{Volovik2003,Barcelo2005}.
\item \textbf{Superfluid vacuum theories} (Sinha, Sivaram, Sudarshan; Zloshchastiev) treat
particles as quasiparticles of a superfluid vacuum \cite{Sinha1976,Zloshchastiev2011}.
\end{itemize}
The \meq{} proposal belongs to this broad lineage. Its defining assumptions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(P1) Single species.} The proposal postulates one type of fundamental quantum, called
the ``minimum energy quantum'' (\meq{}), moving at $c$ and interacting through elastic
collisions. No minimum energy value or spectrum is defined here; the name labels the hypothesis.
\item \textbf{(P2) Matter as bound states.} Observed particles are hypothesized to be collective
or topological configurations of the substrate; no complete particle spectrum is derived here.
\item \textbf{(P3) Gravity as collective field.} The gravitational field is hypothesized to be a
collective substrate degree of freedom sourced by matter and substrate stress-energy.
\item \textbf{(P4) Emergent spacetime.} Operational spacetime and its effective symmetries are
conjectured to arise from the substrate; their derivation is not given here.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What this paper does}

The purpose of this paper is limited to three tasks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{State} the postulates and a schematic kinetic description, distinguishing them from
the microscopic dynamics that remain to be supplied (Sec.~\ref{sec:kinetic}).
\item \textbf{Summarize} relevant observational constraints and explain which assumptions they
would test, without treating compatibility with selected bounds as a global data fit
(Sec.~\ref{sec:exp}).
\item \textbf{Specify} four conditional phenomenological signatures and illustrative estimates for
two of them (Sec.~\ref{sec:pred} and Appendix~\ref{app:quant}).
\end{enumerate}

This is a hypothesis paper, not a completed theory. The equations below do not define a closed
microscopic model, and the proposed parameters are not derived from it. Consequently, the
parameterizations are useful for organizing possible tests but do not demonstrate consistency with
all existing data or establish predictions distinct from every extension of GR or QED.

\section{Conceptual Foundations}\label{sec:concept}

\subsection{Operational spacetime}

A motivating interpretation is operational: measurements of space and time are performed with
physical apparatus, and light signals provide an important means of calibration. This observation
does not by itself derive spacetime symmetries. In particular:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Light-speed invariance} is an empirical property that the proposal must reproduce.
Using light-based apparatus does not alone establish invariance in every inertial frame; this
requires suitable substrate dynamics and an experimentally acceptable low-energy limit.
\item \textbf{A minimum measurable scale} would follow only if the substrate has a finite
correlation or resolution length $\ell_\ast$ and if operational observables inherit it. Neither
property is derived here. This possibility is conceptually distinct from the quantization of
geometric operators in loop quantum gravity \cite{Rovelli2015}.
\item \textbf{An effective metric} can be used to describe propagation in a collective background.
Relating that metric to the metric of GR requires dynamical equations and matter couplings not
specified by the operational interpretation alone \cite{Volovik2003,Donoghue1994}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Postulates}

We state the \meq{} postulates as assumptions to be made quantitative by a future model:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A1 (Substrate).} There exists a universal quantum field $\psi$ whose quanta are
massless ($E = |\mathbf{p}|c$), with an interaction that reduces to elastic scattering at leading
order.
\item \textbf{A2 (Emergence of matter).} The observed particle spectrum, including spin-0,
$\tfrac12$, and 1 excitations, is hypothesized to arise as collective, bound, or topological
configurations of $\psi$.
\emph{The mechanisms discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:fermions} are analogies, not a construction of
the observed fermions from this substrate.}
\item \textbf{A3 (Emergence of gravity).} The gravitational field $g_{\mu\nu}$ is hypothesized to
be a collective mean field of the substrate. A viable realization must specify its self-consistency
condition and show that its low-energy limit includes the Einstein equation.
\item \textbf{A4 (EFT matching).} At energies well below the substrate scale
$E_\ast \sim \hbar c/\ell_\ast$, the framework is required to reproduce the tested predictions of
the Standard Model and GR, with any corrections specified by a matched effective theory.
\end{itemize}
A2 is the least developed link in the chain. Soliton quantization in particular models and
flux--charge composites in 2+1 dimensions illustrate ways in which nontrivial statistics can arise
\cite{Skyrme1962,Goldstone1981,Wilczek1982}; neither supplies the required 3+1-dimensional
construction of the observed chiral gauge spectrum from the proposed single substrate. This
remains an open problem (Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).

\subsection{Classification of observables}

For discussion, one may distinguish \textbf{(i) directly detected quanta} (for example, photons),
\textbf{(ii) particles inferred through their interactions} (including ordinary matter), and
\textbf{(iii) fields inferred from their effects} (including gravitational fields). Replacing
established entities with a substrate would require reproducing their empirical roles; economy of
postulates alone is not evidence in favor of that replacement.

\section{The Kinetic Framework}\label{sec:kinetic}

\subsection{Kinetic ansatz and conservation}

As a schematic starting point, let $f(x,p)$ be a distribution on the future-directed massless
momentum shell, with $p^\mu p_\mu=0$ and $p^0>0$. Choose coordinates $x^0=ct$ and metric signature
$(-,+,+,+)$. A conventional kinetic definition of the substrate stress-energy tensor is
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm sub}^{\mu\nu}(x) \;=\; \int dP\,p^\mu p^\nu f(x,p),
\end{equation}
where $dP$ is the invariant measure on that shell and the normalization of $f$ is chosen so that
$T_{\rm sub}^{\mu\nu}$ has units of energy density. The corresponding Boltzmann equation is written
schematically as
\begin{equation}
p^\mu \partial_\mu f \;=\; C[f,\mathcal{F}],
\end{equation}
where $C$ and the collective field $\mathcal{F}$ have not been specified. For an isolated
substrate collision term, local conservation would require
$\int dP\,p^\nu C[f,\mathcal{F}]=0$; when matter and substrate exchange energy-momentum, only the
combined stress-energy tensor can be conserved. Elasticity of individual collisions does not by
itself imply absence of heating or drag in an interacting medium. Those properties require a
microscopic collision kernel, a stable equilibrium, and a calculation of energy and momentum
transfer. These equations define an ansatz rather than a closed kinetic theory; the Le Sage
objections are constraints to be addressed, not problems resolved by the present postulates.

\subsection{Mean-field self-consistency: induced gravity}\label{sec:induced}

To describe a possible low-energy limit, introduce an effective metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ and write a
generally covariant field equation in derivative-expansion form,
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu}[g] + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
+ \ell_\ast^2 \sum_i a_i H_{\mu\nu}^{(i)} + \cdots
\;=\; \frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}\,T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm total},
\label{eq:einstein}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm total}$ denotes the stress-energy of all low-energy sources, the
$H_{\mu\nu}^{(i)}$ denote tensors of curvature-squared order (dimension $L^{-4}$), and $a_i$ are
dimensionless coefficients. The displayed correction terms therefore have the same dimension
($L^{-2}$) as $G_{\mu\nu}$. This is a possible effective-field-theory organization, motivated by
induced-gravity ideas \cite{Sakharov1968,Donoghue1994}; it does not follow from the kinetic ansatz
above. No microscopic action, regulator, or matching calculation is specified here, so neither
the coefficients nor the coupling to substrate stress-energy are derived. If a model realizes
this limit, it must also explain how the observed Newton constant and cosmological term are fixed.
At this level, the equation identifies requirements rather than established consequences:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Nonlinear gravity must be recovered.} The response to gravitational self-energy
must match precision tests, including lunar laser ranging \cite{Williams2004}. Equation~\eqref{eq:einstein} would have this property at leading order if derived with the required
universal coupling.
\item \textbf{Higher-curvature terms may be present.} If their coefficients are of order unity,
their effects can scale as $(\ell_\ast/\ell)^2$ for curvature radius $\ell$. Neither this
coefficient size nor a relation between $\ell_\ast$ and a Yukawa range is established here; the
short-range constraints in Sec.~\ref{sec:shortrange} apply to a separately parameterized force
law.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fermionic excitations from bosonic constituents}\label{sec:fermions}

One possible route to fermionic excitations in a bosonic theory is soliton quantization with
appropriate topological terms \cite{Skyrme1962,Goldstone1981}. This is an analogy motivating A2,
not a construction within the \meq{} framework: no substrate field content, topological invariant,
or 3+1-dimensional configuration yielding the observed chiral spectrum is provided. The
construction of fermions and their gauge interactions remains an open problem (Sec.~\ref{sec:open}).

\subsection{Lorentz invariance from the substrate}

A low-energy dispersion relation compatible with Lorentz symmetry is a requirement on the
proposal, not a consequence of masslessness of its constituents. As an illustrative
parameterization,
\begin{equation}
E^2 = c^2p^2\left[1+\kappa\left(\frac{pc}{E_\ast}\right)^2+\cdots\right],
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is dimensionless and the omitted terms depend on the effective operators. Existing
tests constrain particular forms of Lorentz violation:
\begin{itemize}
\item Gamma-ray burst timing constrains some specified linear-in-energy dispersion models
\cite{Abdo2009,Vasileiou2013};
\item the absence of vacuum Cherenkov radiation and photon splitting for high-energy photons
bounds certain higher-order structures \cite{Jacobson2003}.
\end{itemize}
The bounds are operator-dependent and cannot be converted into a universal lower limit on
$E_\ast$ without specifying the effective operators and their coefficients
\cite{Myers2005,Bolokhov2007}. This paper does not perform that matching.

\section{Observational Constraints and Requirements}\label{sec:exp}

Any substrate model must satisfy existing constraints. The observations summarized below
illustrate relevant requirements, but they do not constitute a comprehensive parameter-space
analysis: the microscopic couplings needed to translate them into bounds have not been specified.
The discussion therefore distinguishes empirical results from assumptions imposed on a future
realization.

\subsection{Thermodynamics: detailed balance and energy transfer}\label{sec:heat}

The Maxwell--Poincar\'e heating objection to flux models remains relevant
\cite{LeSage2002,Poincare1908}. Elasticity of collisions does not guarantee that matter neither
heats nor experiences drag: energy transfer depends on the distribution, scattering kernel, and
state of the matter. A model would need to demonstrate detailed balance and calculate the net
energy and momentum transfer. The proposed absorption/emission picture is therefore a hypothesis,
not a resolution of the objection. An imbalance could be parameterized as a mass change
(Sec.~\ref{sec:p3}), but the rate and even its existence do not follow from A1.

\subsection{Orbital stability and propagation speed}\label{sec:aberration}

GW170817 constrains the difference between gravitational-wave and electromagnetic propagation
speeds, under assumptions about emission and propagation \cite{GW170817}. A non-covariant
retarded-flux theory of a central force would face Laplace-type aberration. In Lorentz-invariant
field theories, velocity-dependent interaction terms can cancel the leading aberration
\cite{Carlip2000}. Orbital stability and relativistic timing therefore constrain any concrete
realization \cite{Nordtvedt1977}. Writing a covariant effective equation is not sufficient to
establish that its microscopic realization has the required dynamics; the present proposal has not
demonstrated this matching.

\subsection{Universality of free fall and stress-energy coupling}

Universal coupling to stress-energy is a required low-energy property if the framework is to
reproduce GR. Experiments constrain violations of this property, but do not validate its
microscopic origin:
\begin{itemize}
\item MICROSCOPE: $|\eta| < 2.1\times10^{-15}$ for Ti vs.\ Pt-Rh test masses \cite{Touboul2022};
\item neutron interferometry (Colella--Overhauser--Werner \cite{Colella1975}) and the
gravitational quantum states of ultracold neutrons (qBounce \cite{Jenke2014}) demonstrate the
coupling of individual fermions (the neutron) to Earth's gravity, with a phase/Bragg-type response
matching the equivalence principle;
\item the free fall of antihydrogen (ALPHA, 2023 \cite{ALPHA2023}) falls within GR.
\end{itemize}
The E\"ot-Wash results constrain specific additional forces \cite{Lee2020}. The proposal does not
yet derive universal coupling for its hypothetical spinor and bosonic configurations.

\subsection{The gravitational sector and tensor modes}\label{sec:gravitonphoton}

An effective description of gravity resembling GR contains tensor degrees of freedom. Whether
the proposed substrate supports a corresponding collective excitation, and whether it has the
required masslessness and couplings, has not been shown. The analogy to phonons is illustrative
only. Observations constrain the low-energy gravitational sector but do not establish a
spin-2 substrate mode:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Relativistic gravity.} Post-Newtonian tests constrain deviations from GR
\cite{Will2018}; this proposal does not derive those results from its substrate.
\item \textbf{Gravitational waves.} GW170817 constrains the relative propagation speeds of
gravitational and electromagnetic signals, but does not by itself identify their microscopic
degrees of freedom.
\item \textbf{Photon interactions.} Measurements of light-by-light scattering and searches for
vacuum birefringence constrain additional photon interactions
\cite{ATLAS2019,PVLAS2016,Mignani2017}. They do not define a model-independent bound on the
parameter $\zeta$ used below.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Cosmological transparency and photon interactions}

Cosmological observations constrain nonstandard photon energy loss and spectral distortion.
However, the cited observations do not directly determine a universal fractional loss per Hubble
length. In the absence of a specified interaction model, $\xi$ is only a phenomenological
parameter; it is not assigned a numerical observational bound here. Relevant observations include:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Type Ia supernova light curves} exhibit redshift-dependent time dilation
\cite{Goldhaber2001}. This constrains alternatives to expansion but does not by itself measure a
substrate scattering cross-section.
\item \textbf{The Tolman surface-brightness test} probes cosmological expansion and source
evolution \cite{Lubin2001}; interpreting it requires accounting for both.
\item \textbf{Measurements of CMB temperature evolution} constrain energy exchange with the
photon bath.
\item \textbf{The CMB spectrum is close to a blackbody} \cite{Fixsen1996}, limiting spectral
distortions from energy exchange.
\end{enumerate}
Any proposed photon--substrate interaction must be consistent with these observations and with
the standard cosmological interpretation unless an alternative account is supplied. A numerical
limit on $\xi$ requires a specified interaction and propagation model.

\subsection{Dark matter: the phase-dependent gravity conjecture}\label{sec:dm}

A conjecture considered here is \textbf{phase-dependent gravitation}:
gravitational strength tied to the \emph{dynamical phase} of the bound system --- systems in
formation (net inward substrate flux) exhibit a positive anomaly; systems in dissolution exhibit a
negative anomaly; mature systems exhibit none. This sign assignment is qualitative and has not
been derived from a dynamical model. The phenomenological target overlaps with modified-gravity
interpretations of galaxy kinematics, including MOND \cite{McGaugh2016}, but no quantitative
\meq{} mechanism or distinct signature has been derived (Sec.~\ref{sec:p4}). Three classes of
data constrain any such modification:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Bullet Cluster} --- weak-lensing mass peaks displaced from the baryonic gas
\cite{Clowe2006} are commonly interpreted as evidence for a gravitating component that is
collisionless relative to the gas; a phase effect tied only to the baryonic distribution would
need to explain this observation.
\item \textbf{CMB acoustic peaks} --- the third-to-second peak ratio requires a
non-photon-coupled gravitating component at recombination \cite{Planck2020}.
\item \textbf{Big-bang nucleosynthesis and structure formation} --- constrain the
gravitating-to-baryonic density ratio at early times.
\end{enumerate}
A viable alternative to cold dark matter would have to address lensing, the cosmic microwave
background, and structure formation, not only galaxy rotation curves. The proposed phase
dependence has not been shown to meet these requirements or to be distinguishable from baryonic
and environmental effects; it is retained below only as a qualitative research target.

\subsection{Short-range gravity and the substrate scale}\label{sec:shortrange}

Short-range tests constrain Yukawa corrections over the ranges probed by their specific
geometries; the E\"ot-Wash experiment tested separations down to $52\,\mu$m
\cite{Lee2020}. Exclusions in the $(\alpha,\lambda)$ plane are conditional on the force model and
analysis, and do not by themselves bound a generic substrate length. In Sec.~\ref{sec:p1} we use
a Yukawa parameterization as an illustrative, independently testable benchmark. No relation
between $\lambda$, $\alpha$, and $\ell_\ast$ has been derived for this proposal.

\section{Conditional Tests and the Experimental Program}\label{sec:pred}

\subsection{Status of the proposed signatures}

The preceding sections state a substrate hypothesis and identify requirements for a viable
realization; they do not derive a closed theory or establish new effects. The following four
items are therefore conditional tests: two standard phenomenological parameterizations, one
exchange-motivated ansatz, and one qualitative astrophysical conjecture. Their status and
limitations are stated separately below.

\subsection{Test 1: a short-range Yukawa benchmark}\label{sec:p1}

As a benchmark for short-range tests, consider a Yukawa correction to Newtonian gravity,
\begin{equation}
V(r) \;=\; -\frac{G m_1 m_2}{r}\Bigl[\,1 + \alpha\, e^{-r/\lambda}\Bigr],
\label{eq:yukawa}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha$ is dimensionless and $\lambda$ is a range parameter. This parameterization is not
derived from the \meq{} postulates: no relation between $\lambda$, $\ell_\ast$, and $\alpha$ has
been established. Existing short-range measurements constrain parts of the $(\alpha,\lambda)$
plane for their assumed force models \cite{Lee2020}.

\textbf{Quantitative estimate (details in Appendix~\ref{app:quant}).} For two parallel tungsten
plates at gap $d$ the Yukawa pressure is
\begin{equation}
P_Y(d) \;=\; 2\pi\, G\, \alpha\, \lambda^{2} \rho_1 \rho_2\, e^{-d/\lambda},
\label{eq:pyuk}
\end{equation}
For uniform, thick plates this expression gives the pressure per unit area. At
$\alpha=1$, $\lambda=39\,\mu$m, and $d=52\,\mu$m it is approximately
$6.3\times10^{-11}\,$Pa for tungsten. This idealized pressure is not an experimental sensitivity
or a reproduction of the E\"ot-Wash analysis, which depends on the apparatus geometry and
systematics. For illustrative parallel plates with $A=100\,$cm$^2$ and gap $40\,\mu$m, a force
noise of $10^{-16}$--$10^{-17}\,$N would correspond to
$|\alpha|_{\min}\approx 10^{-3}$--$10^{-4}$ at $\lambda=20\,\mu$m in this idealized model
(Appendix~\ref{app:quant}). This estimate omits finite geometry, alignment, surface, and
background uncertainties. Casimir forces are much larger at these separations; distinguishing
them requires a realistic geometry and systematic-error analysis, not separation scaling alone.
\textbf{Conditional test:} the Yukawa benchmark can be searched for as experiments improve at
short range. A null result constrains $(\alpha,\lambda)$, but does not by itself bound
$\ell_\ast$ without a derived matching relation.

\subsection{Test 2: an additional photon--field interaction}\label{sec:p2}

An additional contribution to photon propagation in an external field can be parameterized by
$\zeta$, defined here as the fractional change in the leading weak-field QED birefringence
coefficient. This is a phenomenological parameter, not a derived substrate interaction. Explicitly, we define
\begin{equation}
\Delta n=(1+\zeta)\Delta n_{\rm QED},\qquad
\psi_{\rm new}=\zeta\,\psi_{\rm QED}.
\end{equation}
Existing laboratory and astrophysical observations constrain particular birefringence models;
they do not provide a model-independent bound on this $\zeta$.

\textbf{Illustrative estimate (Appendix~\ref{app:quant}).} Using the weak-field coefficient
$\Delta n_{\rm QED}\simeq 4.0\times10^{-24}B^2$ with $B$ in tesla and
$\psi=N_{\rm eff}\pi\Delta n L/\lambda_{\rm light}$, an idealized PVLAS-like configuration
($B=2.5\,$T, $L=1\,$m, $N_{\rm eff}=2.5\times10^5$) gives
$\psi_{\rm QED}\simeq1.8\times10^{-11}\,$rad. A projected 13\,T, 25\,m configuration with
$N_{\rm eff}=6.4\times10^4$ gives about $3.2\times10^{-9}\,$rad. These estimates assume the
quoted effective pass counts and neglect backgrounds and instrumental systematics; they are not
performance forecasts. A residual proportional to $\zeta$ could be sought after independently
calibrating the QED contribution. A null result would constrain this parameterization only and
would not imply a specific microscopic coupling.

\subsection{Test 3: a mass-drift ansatz}\label{sec:p3}

An exchange model might permit a net energy transfer between a body and the substrate. As a
phenomenological ansatz, suppose the fractional inertial-mass drift is proportional to the local
gravitational acceleration magnitude $g$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{m}\frac{dm}{dt} \;=\; \eta\,\frac{g}{c},
\label{eq:etadrift}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ is dimensionless and signed. This relation is not derived from energy-momentum
exchange; a microscopic model would have to specify the transfer rate, the meaning of $g$ in a
relativistic setting, and the conservation law for the combined system. At Earth's surface,
$g/c\simeq3.3\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, so the ansatz provides a normalization for a
potential test, not a bound or prediction for $\eta$.

\textbf{Conditional test:} a laboratory search could monitor a well-defined mass-sensitive
observable over time and compare it with an independently calibrated gravitational environment.
The experimental sensitivity and interpretation would depend on the measurement protocol and on
whether the proposed drift is common-mode or composition-dependent. Binary-pulsar orbital decay \cite{Kramer2021} and clock-ratio measurements cannot be translated
into a bound on $\eta$ without a model of how the drift changes the orbit, clock frequencies, and
system energy budget. A null result would
constrain the specified phenomenological relation only after these mappings are supplied.
Equation~\eqref{eq:etadrift} is not a generic prediction of induced-gravity effective field theory.

\subsection{Test 4: a phase-correlated galactic signal}\label{sec:p4}

The conjecture in Sec.~\ref{sec:dm} suggests looking for a correlation between galaxy kinematic
residuals and independently defined evolutionary indicators, such as specific star-formation
rate, at fixed stellar mass and environment. No quantitative relation, sign, or amplitude is
specified, and these indicators are not themselves a definition of a dynamical phase. A useful
test would require a preregistered observable and a model that accounts for baryonic physics,
selection effects, lensing, and the predictions of competing models. Rotation-curve and
weak-lensing data across redshift could eventually provide relevant comparisons, but present
surveys do not, by themselves, establish this signature. Any account replacing dark matter must
also address the CMB and structure formation.

\section{Open Problems and Limitations}\label{sec:open}

The proposal is incomplete in several respects that prevent it from being treated as a predictive
theory:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Microscopic dynamics.} No action, Hamiltonian, collision kernel, or well-defined
substrate state is supplied. Consequently the kinetic equation is schematic, and conservation,
stability, detailed balance, and the absence of unacceptable heating or drag have not been shown.
\item \textbf{Matter and gauge fields.} No construction of the observed fermion spectrum, its
chiral structure, or the gauge group $\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{SU}(3)$ is
given. Examples of topological excitations in other models do not establish this construction.
\item \textbf{Gravity and matching.} The effective field equation is an assumed low-energy form.
There is no derivation of the metric, Newton's constant, universal matter coupling, tensor-mode
spectrum, or higher-curvature coefficients from the proposed substrate.
\item \textbf{Phenomenological parameters.} The Yukawa parameters, $\zeta$, and $\eta$ are
introduced independently; no microscopic calculation relates them to one another or to
$\ell_\ast$. The numerical estimates are therefore sensitivity illustrations, not predictions
from a specified parameter set.
\item \textbf{Cosmology and galactic phenomenology.} There is no quantitative treatment of
inflation, baryogenesis, the CMB anisotropy spectrum, or structure formation. The phase-dependent
galactic conjecture has no specified dynamics and is not an alternative cosmological model.
\item \textbf{Parameter constraints.} Existing observations constrain specific interactions and
force laws. The bounds quoted for those models cannot be identified with limits on
$\ell_\ast$, $\zeta$, $\eta$, or a photon-loss parameter without a matching calculation.
\end{enumerate}

Accordingly, the present manuscript should be read as a research proposal. Its central idea---a
single light-speed substrate with emergent matter and gravity---is not yet supported by a closed
dynamics or a demonstrated low-energy limit. Progress requires first specifying a consistent
microscopic model and deriving at least one distinctive, quantitatively controlled observable.
The illustrative tests in Sec.~\ref{sec:pred} can then be assessed against that model.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented the Minimum Energy Quantum framework as a phenomenological proposal for a
single light-speed substrate from which matter and gravity might emerge. A schematic kinetic
equation and an effective gravitational field equation make explicit some structures a future
model would need, but neither is derived from microscopic dynamics here. We also identified four
conditional tests: short-range Yukawa forces, additional vacuum birefringence, a possible
exchange-associated mass drift, and a qualitative galaxy-evolution correlation. The first two
are standard parameterizations with idealized signal estimates; the latter two lack a quantitative
observable-to-parameter mapping. Existing experiments provide important constraints, but the
discussion here is not a global consistency analysis and does not establish the substrate
hypothesis. A decisive next step is to specify the microscopic model, derive its effective
couplings and conservation laws, and then reassess these tests with realistic data and
uncertainties.
